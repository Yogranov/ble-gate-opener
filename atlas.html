Drive to the gate.
It opens.

A small unit in the glove box listens for a beacon on the gate and presses your gate remote for you, once, at the right moment. After setup there is nothing to do.

This atlas covers how it works and how it is built. Installing a unit? The technician manual, which folds up to go inside the case, is all you need.

The rule

Press once when the car has moved in the last minute and can hear the beacon.

Starting the car never opens the gate.

Firmware 2.0.1. Status on 2 November 2026: one real day in a car. The presses came at the right moments, and none at ignition, but the remote's battery was low, so the gate has not yet opened from a press. On a car screen's USB port the unit often fails several times before it starts. On a good phone charger it has not failed in 4 starts, so it should run from one, and that needs more drives to confirm. The motion thresholds have their first real data, and are still not settled. Understand

What it does

YogiGate opens a gate without anyone touching a remote. A battery beacon on the gate keeps announcing itself over Bluetooth. The unit in your car listens for it and watches for movement. When the two line up, it presses the button of a remote that is already paired with the gate.

The remote is an ordinary gate remote with two wires soldered across its button, so the gate itself needs no changes. The unit needs no internet and no app. Its settings live on a small web page, on a Wi-Fi hotspot that it starts when you ask.

How it decides

The parking spot this was built for is 13 m from the gate, close enough to hear the beacon while still parked. Signal strength alone cannot separate parked from leaving, and an earlier version opened the gate before the driver was ready. So a press needs two things at once.

Beacon not heardBeacon heard
Car has not movedNothingParked out of range.NothingGetting ready in the spot.
Car moved in the last minuteNothing yetDriving toward the gate.Press onceHolds the button for 2 s, then stays quiet.

Heard means at or above the set point. By default the set point is Any, so a single advert from the paired beacon is enough.

The four states

How it knows the car is moving

An MPU6050 sensor reports 50 times a second. At every start the unit works out which way is down, normally within 2 s, so any mounting position works. It reports no motion until then. After that, either of these counts as driving.

15° in 3 sA turnA car leaving a perpendicular spot has to turn, and door slams do not produce one.
40 mg for 0.8 sA steady pushSideways or forward force without a break. Any dip below it starts the time again.

Fast tilting, such as opening the glove box, pauses the push check for a second so it does not look like driving. A loose unit can still fool it, which is why it has to be fixed in place.

    Set up

    Build the unit

    The car unit is hand-built on a 50 × 70 mm matrix board. This section gives the overview. The hole-by-hole drawings, build order and tests are in hardware/board-layout.html, and the reasons behind each part are in hardware/design.md.

    You needMuseLab ESP32-C6 board with two USB-C ports. The ESP32-C6-DevKitC-1 has the same GPIOs, but its size and pin order would not fit this layout. MPU6050 module. P30N06LE MOSFET, or an IRLB8743, because an IRF520 or IRF540N may not turn on at 3.3 V. A 220 Ω and a 10 kΩ resistor. A gate remote already paired with your gate. A BLE beacon, normally an iBeacon. Matrix board, headers, thin wire, a strip of Kapton or electrical tape, and hot glue.
    OptionalAn active buzzer, under about 20 mA at 3.3 V. Leaving it off breaks nothing. A 6 × 6 mm tactile button, which is how you start the hotspot from outside the case. The BOOT button on the C6 does the same, but it is out of reach inside the case, and so a unit with no tactile button needs USB or an open case.

    The board

    How the press works

    Do not swap the two resistors. They form a divider on the gate. Swapped, it reaches under 0.1 V and the remote will not press. Check them with a meter, because the colour bands can mislead.

    Find the remote's (+) wire with a meter, battery in. The wire under the red probe when the reading is positive is (+). Reversed, the remote acts as if its button is held. Wire colours do not tell you. On the BFT remote this was built around, the black wire is (+).

    Lay the MOSFET flat, print up, on a strip of tape. Its metal tab is the drain. The first unit was built print down with different wiring, so never flip a MOSFET on a board wired for the other way, or the remote will not press.

    Keep to the pins below. GPIO 18 to 23 are pulled up during reset, which could press the remote at every ignition. A button on GPIO 9 that is held at power-on stops the unit starting.

    The printed case

    Two printed parts snap together with no screws, about 75 × 64 × 46 mm when closed. Print them in ASA, which keeps its shape in a parked car's heat. PETG does for test prints, and PLA softens. A printed copy of the technician manual, folded to 60 × 33 mm, fits flat under the top. Measure the assumed sizes before printing. hardware/case/README.md has the sizes to check, the print settings and the assembly steps.

    Flash and pair

    1. Install ESP-IDF v6.1 
      winget install Espressif.EIM-CLI
eim install -i v6.1 -t esp32c6
    2. Build and flash

      Use a terminal with the ESP-IDF environment active. Plug into the C6's native USB port, marked ESP32C6, and use your own COM port.

      idf.py build
idf.py -p COM3 flash
    3. Start the hotspot

      Triple-click the button. You hear three short beeps. Its name is YogiGate-XXXX and its password is password. Over USB, this does the same and prints both. It needs pip install pyserial and assumes COM3, so add --port COM5 before the command if yours differs.

      python tools/yg_serial.py wifi
    4. Pair the beacon, one of three ways
      Type its MACFor a beacon already on the gateUsually the easiest. On the page, Beacon tab, Type a MAC address. Or over USB with the command below. No trip to the gate needed.
      Choose from nearbyWhen you are in rangeBeacon tab, Scan. It lists nearby iBeacons with live signal, major and minor. Tap Use on yours. Automatic presses wait until you finish.
      Hold it closeBefore it goes on the gateHold the button for 3 s, or tap Start on the page, then hold the beacon against the unit. Only iBeacons count. It gives up after 60 s and keeps the old beacon.
      python tools/yg_serial.py pair c5:b0:e6:53:59:02

      To find a MAC, python tools/yg_serial.py devices 10 lists every Bluetooth device heard in 10 s. Phones and watches cannot be paired, because their address keeps changing. Pairing a new beacon resets the set point to Any.

    5. Test before it goes in the car

      Power-cycle the unit several times and watch the remote's LED, which must stay off at every start. Then tap Press the remote on the page. You hear one short beep and the remote's LED should light for 2 s. Shake the unit and watch Live motion move.

    Fit it in the car

    • Power it from the ignition. Use a USB source that turns off with the car. The unit expects to lose power with no warning and does not mind it.
    • Fix it so it cannot slide or tumble. Any orientation works. A loose unit can look like driving. The case takes a strip of Velcro or Dual Lock on its Cover, with acrylic adhesive, because the rubber adhesive on ordinary Velcro can let go in a hot car.
    • Prove the remote's range first. Inside the closed glove box, the remote has to open the gate from the parking spot. If it does not, move it off the board on longer wires. Nothing else changes, though the printed case would need an opening for them.
    • Turn on Debug mode for the first weeks. It records raw captures, which make tuning much easier. Turn it off once things are settled, because it also keeps the hotspot on while driving.
    • Keep another remote within reach. With the unit in a closed glove box there is no quick way to press by hand while driving.

    A second unit takes the same steps and starts with the same default password, so change it if you want them to differ. It can share the same beacon, so type the same MAC. It needs no tuning for a different parking spot, because the motion check does that job.

    Use

    Every day

    Nothing. Start the car and drive. The gate should open by itself about 2 s after you pull out, or as soon as the beacon is heard if you are arriving.

    What you will not noticeThe gate opens with no beep. The LED stays off, and the hotspot stays off until you ask for it.
    When something feels wrongOpen the page when you are next parked and rate that trip on the Logs tab. The unit cannot see the gate, so your rating is the only record of whether it really opened.

    The web page

    The page starts simple: the state, live signal and motion, pairing, the set point, and the buzzer and LED switches. Turn on Advanced mode at the bottom of the Settings tab for everything else: all the settings, Reset to defaults, each sound, firmware upload, Reset logs, factory reset and the debug tools. Your phone remembers the choice.

    1. Triple-click the button

      The hotspot only starts when you ask, and you hear three short beeps. It does not start with the car.

    2. Join the hotspot from your phone's Wi-Fi settings

      Its name is YogiGate-XXXX, ending in four characters from the unit's address. The password is password. Your phone may warn that the network has no internet, so choose to stay connected.

    3. Open http://192.168.4.1

      Type the http:// part too. There is no sign-in page, and the Wi-Fi password is the only protection.

    The default password is public. While the hotspot is on, anyone in Wi-Fi range who knows it can press your remote or upload firmware. The hotspot is only on after someone presses the button, and Change password on the Settings tab replaces it if you want.

    The button

    The BOOT button on the C6 and the optional tactile button do the same things. In the printed case you press a flexible tab in the top, flush with the surface so that things lying in the glove box do not set it off.

    Sounds and LED

    The buzzer is on by default, and answers people. It sounds when someone does something on the unit, or when something is wrong. It stays quiet when the gate opens by itself, so you will not hear it on a normal trip. An active buzzer has one fixed tone, so each sound is a rhythm.

    To silence it, turn off Buzzer on the Settings tab. Buzzer type is active by default, which suits the buzzer in this build. If you try passive with an active buzzer fitted and switch back, restart the unit or it stays silent.

    The LED

    The LED is dim on purpose and stays off in normal use. In the printed case it cannot be seen yet, because no window has been added for it, so listen for the sounds there. The dots below blink at the real speed.

    Tune and fix

    Settings

    The defaults are meant to work right after pairing. The one you are most likely to change is the set point.

    Reading the signal

    Leave the set point on Any unless you have a reason not to. It gives the most warning. Raise it for two reasons: the gate opens as you drive past on the street, or a press comes from farther away than the remote can reach. Set too strict, a weak day can leave you stopped at a closed gate, which the panel under Watch a trip shows with Weak and −90 dBm together.

    To set it, park where the press should happen and tap Set here, or double-click the button. It stores the strongest advert of the last 3 s, or 10 s with the hotspot on. You can also type a value.

    Logs

    A trip is one power-on period, from ignition on to ignition off. Trips are numbered rather than dated, because the unit has no clock across power cuts. Each time you open the page, it stores your phone's time as a marker, so dates can be estimated. By calculation from real record sizes, at 4 trips a day the 2 MB log holds about 6 months of 20-minute trips, and the oldest data is overwritten. Debug captures hold about 90 events, taken only at the start of a trip and near the beacon, so a long drive does not overwrite them. Reset logs, on the Logs tab in Advanced mode, erases it and starts counting trips from 1 again.

    Rate your trips

    The unit cannot see the gate. When something feels wrong, open the trip on the Logs tab and tap the rating that fits: Good, Too early, Too late, Didn't open or Opened for no reason.

    The chart under each trip is the one in Watch a trip: signal as a line, with one marker where motion was detected and one where the remote was pressed.

    Have them reviewed

    1. Download the logs

      Tap Download everything on the Logs tab. Or fetch them over USB with the command below. The files show when you come and go, so logs/ stays out of git.

      python tools/yg_logs.py fetch --port COM3
    2. Give the file to a Claude session in this repo

      Ask it to review them. It looks for motion while parked, how much warning each press gave, whether bad days show a weaker signal, and whether your ratings agree with the data.

    3. Upload the new firmware

      Changes come back as a firmware file, which you upload from the System tab.

    Or look yourself

    python tools/yg_logs.py summary logs/<file>.ndjson
python tools/yg_logs.py replay logs/<file>.ndjson --trip 12 --set motion_accel_mg=60

    Replay runs a real trip through the current logic with a changed setting, starting from the settings that trip really had. It works best with raw captures from Debug mode. The format is in docs/log-format.md.

    Troubleshooting

    What you seeWhat to do
    The gate
    The gate did not openOpen that trip on the Logs tab. With a press marker, the unit did its job, so look at the remote: its battery, then its range from the glove box. The firmware cannot see either. With no press marker, the beacon was not heard at the set point, so try Any and check the signal on the Status tab. Rate the trip either way.
    It opened while parkedCheck the unit cannot slide or tumble. Rate the trip Opened for no reason. If it happens again, turn on Debug mode so the next one is captured, then have the logs reviewed. Also look for the sensor failure below.
    It opens when driving past on the streetRaise the set point until the street no longer reaches it.
    It presses too far away and the gate does not respondThe Bluetooth range is longer than the remote's. Raise the set point so that presses happen within the remote's reach.
    It did not press again at the gateAfter a press it stays quiet until the beacon has been gone for 1 minute, or the car has been still for 2 minutes. Press by hand from the button or the page, or use your other remote.
    It pressed twice after a stallA known limit. The unit cannot tell how long the power was off, so a restart in range can press again.
    The page and hotspot
    The hotspot is not thereIt only starts when you ask. Triple-click the button and listen for three short beeps, or run python tools/yg_serial.py wifi. It turns off 5 minutes later if nobody has joined.
    The page will not openJoin from your phone's Wi-Fi settings by hand, stay connected when it warns about no internet, and type http://192.168.4.1 in full. On the Android phone it was tested with, this works with mobile data left on.
    The page says the unit is not answeringIt may be busy with a download, or your phone left the hotspot. The server handles one request at a time, so a press tapped during a log download or firmware upload can wait a minute or more. Automatic presses do not wait.
    The hotspot starts by itself, or there are no beepsA flash keeps saved settings, so this unit still has its old ones. Use Reset to defaults on the Settings tab in Advanced mode. It keeps the beacon and logs.
    Forgot the hotspot passwordIt is password unless someone changed it. Otherwise connect USB and run python tools/yg_serial.py wifi, which prints it.
    The hardware
    Red double blink, or a motion sensor warning on the pageThe unit is on the fixed threshold. The page says whether the sensor is not found, giving wrong readings, not answering or frozen. Check its four wires. It recovers by itself after 5 s of good readings.
    The remote's LED stays onIts (+) and (−) wires are swapped, so the MOSFET's internal diode is holding the button. Swap them.
    Press the remote does nothing to the remoteCheck the resistors are not swapped, and that the MOSFET lies the way its board is wired for: print up on boards built to the current layout, print down on the first unit. With the unit powered, its gate should reach about 3.2 V during a press.
    Pairing by holding close keeps timing outThe beacon must read at least −45 dBm and beat every other beacon by 15 dB for 2 s. Hold it right against the unit, or type its MAC instead.
    No beep when the gate opensThat is correct. It only sounds when someone does something on the unit, or when something is wrong.
    The buzzer is silentTry Beep on the System tab. Check Buzzer is on and Buzzer type is active in Settings, and restart if it ever beeped as passive.
    Three long beepsThe motion sensor failed. See the red double blink above.
    A firmware upload failedThe previous firmware is still running, so nothing is lost. If the new one fails its self-check or loses power in its first 30 s, the unit also goes back to the previous one by itself.

    Upkeep

    Every yearReplace the remote's batteryThe firmware cannot see a dead one, so do it on a schedule. The BFT remote this was built around takes a 12 V 23A. Pull the case off its Velcro and turn it upside down, lever the Cover off with a coin at the notch at either end, and lift it away with the remote on it. Keep the Body upside down while it is off, because only the Cover holds the board in.
    When neededUpdate the firmwareBuild with idf.py build, then on the System tab choose build/yogigate.bin and tap Upload. Keep the power on for 30 s while it checks itself. If the check fails, the previous firmware comes back by itself. Uploading needs only the Wi-Fi password.
    RarelyResetReset to defaults, on the Settings tab in Advanced mode, puts every setting back, including the hotspot name and password, and keeps the beacon, logs and the motion sensor's saved gyro zero. A flash keeps saved settings, so use this after an update that changes a default. Factory reset, by holding the button for 15 s, on the System tab, or with factory-reset over USB, also erases the beacon and all logs, and can take a minute on full logs.
    When it goes quietThe beaconIt runs on its own battery on the gate, and the unit only knows it has stopped hearing it. If the signal drops for good on the Status tab, check the beacon first.

    Not proven yet

    The logic has been through several rounds of review, and as of 2 November 2026 it has one real day in a car behind it. The old unit stays in the car until the new one has passed these.

    Tested
    • The logic tests on a PC
    • Every bench check on the C6: the API, firmware update and rollback, full logs, repeated restarts
    • The soldered board over USB: MPU6050, MOSFET, active buzzer and tactile button
    • The page on a real phone over the real hotspot
    • The sounds, on-request hotspot and live motion, on a PC and compiled for the C6
    • The new firmware on the real unit: live motion values, all 7 sounds heard on the buzzer, Reset to defaults, Reset logs, the hotspot staying off at a start, and a phone on the page staying connected while the unit scans
    • The 15 s factory reset on the real unit, with the hold faked in firmware: a 14 s hold erases nothing, and at 15 s it sounds, erases the beacon, settings and logs, and comes back clean
    • One day of driving, about an hour: a press on the way out and on the way back, each after the car moved, and none at ignition or away from the beacon. Each press is counted as an opening, because the remote's battery was low
    • A triple-click starting the hotspot, in the car
    • Two prints of the case: the board, remote and C6 fit inside, though the Cover still closed a little loosely with the parts in
    Not tested
    • A real press opening the gate, and how far the remote reaches from the glove box
    • The board layout of 5 November 2026, for the next units: the MOSFET print up with its gate wiring changed, and the motion sensor and buzzer one column over. Nothing has been built to it yet, and the first unit keeps the earlier layout
    • Starting reliably on a phone charger, over many drives. On the first day, on a car screen's USB port, 4 of 6 power-ups failed 15 to 117 times first, as brownouts. In a later test the screen's ports failed up to 7 times, and a good charger none of 4 starts, only one of them with the engine. No voltage was measured
    • Holding the real button for 15 s, and hearing its sound on the buzzer. On the bench the hold was faked in firmware
    • The targets below: one day is far too few, and door slams have not been counted. On the first drives, pull-outs were detected about 4 s after the car moved
    • The remote's range when it sits against the board
    • This version of the case, whose stronger hooks, snugger remote clips and 0.5 mm button gap have not been printed
    • The case in ASA through a summer of heat, and its effect on the signal
    • The automatic press being silent, by ear
    • How loud the buzzer is in a closed glove box, now that the case has no slots for it
