YogiGate board layout

The car unit on a 50 × 70 mm matrix board, rows A to X and columns 01 to 18. Q11 means row Q, column 11. Drawn to scale from measurements and photos of the real parts. The C6 lies across the board, with its USB ports at the column 18 edge.

Check these before soldering

  1. Board direction. Hold the board with A at the top and 01 at the left. That face is the parts side. If the numbers run the other way, ignore them and count from the left. Hole names always come from this side. The back of the board has its own printed letters, which run the other way: A on the back is X here, and H on the back is Q here. The column numbers match.
  2. C6 fit. Push the header pins into the board, without solder. The pin rows should land in rows G and Q, from column 01 to column 15, with the antenna past the column 01 edge and the USB ports at the column 18 edge. Row Q should read from the left: GND, 3V3, RST, 4, 5, 6, 7, 0, 1, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 5V.
  3. USB ports. With the C6 on its headers, the USB cable should plug into both ports without touching anything.
  4. Active buzzer. Put the meter on mA between 3.3 V and buzzer +, with buzzer − to GND. An active buzzer beeps steadily, and a passive one only clicks. It should read under about 20 mA. Then jumper it to GPIO11 and GND, set its type to active on the unit's web page, and use the Beep button. It should be loud enough with the glove box closed.
  5. Buzzer pins. Its pins should fit holes C15 and C18, with 2 holes between them.
  6. Remote range. Hold the remote against the back of the board and press its button by hand from the parking spot, with the board in the glove box. If the gate doesn't open reliably, keep the remote off the board on longer wires. Everything else stays the same.
  7. Button pins. Its legs span 4 holes one way, with 2 holes between them, and 3 holes the other way, with 1 between them. Turn it so its legs go in R16, R18, U16 and U18: 4 holes from row R to row U, and 3 holes from column 16 to column 18. Once soldered, it should measure about 6.5 mm from the board's top to the top of its stem, which is what the case allows for.
  8. Resistors. Use a meter, because the blue resistor's bands read as 220 Ω from one end and 10 kΩ from the other. The one for row S must read about 220 Ω, and the one for column 10 about 10 kΩ. Anything else there, and the remote will not press reliably.
  9. MOSFET. Before gluing it, prove which leg is which. Touch all three legs together, then hold it with the print facing you and the legs down. On diode test, red on the right leg and black on the middle leg reads about 0.5 to 0.7 V. The left leg reads open to the middle leg, both ways. That makes the left leg G. Then connect 3.3 V to G and GND to S. With red on D and black on S, it should read about 0 Ω. Join G to S, and it should read open.

If check 2, 3, 5 or 7 fails, stop before soldering, because the layout has to change. If the buzzer in check 4 is too quiet or draws too much, the firmware can first raise the pin's drive to 40 mA.

Build order

Hover or tap a row to pick out that wire in the solder side drawing. Use thin insulated wire, except where a bare offcut or a part's lead is noted.

#WhatHolesSide

Small parts

  • 1 × 220 Ω and 1 × 10 kΩ resistor
  • 2 × 15-pin female headers, 2 × 15-pin male headers for the C6, 1 × 8-pin male header for the MPU6050
  • 1 × 6.5 × 6 mm tactile button, 6 mm tall
  • Thin insulated wire, hot glue

MOSFET legs

With the print facing you and the legs down, the order is G, D, S from the left. Mark G with a pen, then bend the legs just past their wide part. The MOSFET lies with its print facing the board, because the metal tab is the drain and must not rest on the pads. With the print up, G and S swap in these holes, and the remote will not press. As fitted, you cannot read the print, and G is the leg nearest the C6.

Before plugging in the C6

  • G14 to G15 (GND to 5V) is not shorted. This is the only place 5V sits next to GND.
  • Q1 to Q2 (GND to 3V3) is not shorted.
  • Q10 to Q11 is not shorted. GPIO8 is pulled up on the C6, so a bridge here could press the remote at every start.
  • These neighbours are not shorted either: G1 to G2, G2 to G3, G3 to G4, G13 to G14, Q2 to Q3, Q6 to Q7, Q7 to Q8, Q11 to Q12 and Q12 to Q13.
  • E5 to E6 (MPU6050 VCC to GND) is not shorted.
  • S8 to S12 reads about 220 Ω.
  • T9 to V9 reads about 10 kΩ.
  • V9 to Q1 reads about 0 Ω.
  • With the remote connected and its battery in, its LED is off. If it is on, (+) and (−) are swapped.

With the C6 plugged in

  • Before connecting USB, Q15 to Q1 (5V to GND) is not shorted.
  • Flash the current firmware, because older builds don't read the button on GPIO7. An old build folder needs idf.py reconfigure once.
  • Power cycle several times. The remote LED must stay off at every start.
  • Press the remote from the unit's web page. Its LED lights, and U9 to V9 reads under about 50 mV. The remote really transmits, so test away from the gate.
  • On the unit's web page, turn the buzzer on, set its type to active, and then use the Beep button. If it already beeped as the passive type, restart the unit first, or it stays silent.
  • Click the external button once. It presses the remote.
  • The unit's web page shows the motion sensor as found.
  • In the car, the beacon signal has not dropped compared with the C6 alone.
